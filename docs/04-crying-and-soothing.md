# 04 — Crying & Soothing

> Status: Draft v0.2 · 2026-11-17
> See [02](02-needs-cues-and-actions.md) for needs, [03](03-realistic-frequency-tables.md) for gameplay timings and [09](09-education-curriculum.md) for the reviewed teaching path.

## 1. Distress and need state

Each actionable need has its own ID and phase. Open needs contribute pressure. A distress value drives the visible and audible intensity for the child as a whole. It can rise with several simultaneous needs, an unsettled evening, or a change in the environment. Use continuous pressure between events and named levels the UI can show with animation, text and optional sound.

An action can reduce distress and address a need, or provide only comfort. For example, holding can help a hungry child settle enough to attempt a feed. Hunger remains open until fed or handed off. When a feed ends at fullness, credit the response. Distress can outlast a resolved need if the child is tired or the scenario includes a sustained bout.

An unanswered routine need can reach a game-only escalation cap and be handled by a helper. Record what the helper did and how the need resolved. Use the cap to bound reminders and care simulations. A safety or health concern opens the corresponding reviewed help-seeking branch.

## 2. Causes of crying

| Possible need | Useful context | Care available in the game |
|---|---|---|
| Hunger | Rooting, hands to mouth, time since an accepted feed | Offer a feed. If needed, hold and settle briefly first |
| Wet or dirty diaper | Squirming, recent change or stool event | Check the diaper and change it if needed |
| Gas or discomfort after a feed | Pulling away or drawing legs up after a feed | Pause, hold and try to burp if needed |
| Tiredness | Yawning, turning away, recent sleep history | Reduce stimulation and settle to a safe sleep space |
| Too much stimulation | Noise, bright room, visitors, looking away | Move to a quieter place and respond gently |
| Connection or boredom | Awake and alone, calms when engaged | Hold, talk or play gently while awake |
| Temperature or clothing discomfort | Context, clothing layers, a tag or wet clothes | Check comfort, adjust layers or remove the irritant |
| Persistent fussing | Simulation can include prolonged crying after routine checks | Offer comfort and consider a break or help. A later reviewed scenario can introduce illness or pain cues |
| Worry or frustration | Separation, a transition, a difficult task | Reassure and respond in age-appropriate ways |

Cues overlap. Show the same cry intensity for different causes. Descriptions such as “crying louder and turning away” work across sound, animation and TalkBack. The right action comes from context and checks.

## 3. Observation loop

1. Observe body language, state changes and the recent care log.
2. Check a plausible cause, such as the diaper or when they last fed.
3. Respond with a short action or begin a timed care session.
4. Show what changed. Distress can fall while another need remains.
5. Debrief with the simulated cause or causes and the evidence the player could observe. Give credit for checks, reasonable comfort and safe help-seeking, including when no action stops crying immediately.

**Replayable example.** The child turns away, cries harder and has a dirty diaper. The player checks and changes it. Fussing eases but rooting continues. The player offers a feed and stops at fullness. The wake finishes with safe settling. Count two need episodes, one wake and separate completed actions.

## 4. Soothing toolkit

| Technique | In the game | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Hold and talk softly | Tap to pick up, choose a quiet voice | Offers immediate connection |
| Slow rocking or humming | Guided button or rhythm control | Gradual comfort. Motion or voice can be optional later inputs |
| Quiet and dim surroundings | Scene control | Helps when there has been overstimulation |
| Feed or change | Timed care flow with cue feedback | Can resolve the corresponding need |
| Check and burp | Short care step during or after a feed | Helps with the virtual baby's modeled discomfort |
| Resettle with a safe transfer | Prepare the sleep space and place the child on their back | Ends a sleep-preparation step and enables rest |
| Swaddle or offer a pacifier | Later age-appropriate practice | Optional comfort tools with safety gating and reviewed instruction |

A technique has a time to work and a limited effect for each cause. Make gentle actions accessible from the first trial. Give a cue when an action is helping and allow a player to change course as new information appears. The simulation has no required sequence of soothing tricks.

## 5. Sustained crying scenarios

Seed an evening with prolonged fussing or allow the player to choose one in the Practice Room. Present common care checks and show partial feedback, even when the scenario keeps some distress. A review of the care log explains that some crying can persist despite responsive care. Include a way to stop audio and take a break. [AAP guidance on responding to cries](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Responding-to-Your-Babys-Cries.aspx)

## 6. “I need a break”

Offer three clear controls. **Quiet** mutes in-app sound. **Hand off** transfers care while the run continues. **Pause** immediately freezes the run and cancels future alerts. Handoff and pause are always available.

A teaching card can guide the player through putting the baby on their back in a clear, safe crib, stepping away briefly if overwhelmed, asking for help and checking back. The card teaches never to shake a baby. [AAP guidance on caregiver breaks](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/crying-colic/Pages/Calming-a-Fussy-Baby.aspx), [NIH Safe to Sleep](https://safetosleep.nichd.nih.gov/reduce-risk/reduce)

## 7. Safety gates

| Situation | In-game behavior |
|---|---|
| Unsafe sleep setup | Show the issue and provide a safe setup before settling |
| Swaddling after rolling attempts | Block the action and offer an age-appropriate alternative |
| Missed presence prompt | Pause or offer to resume the session. Keep completed steps and avoid inferring that the player fell asleep |
| Player chooses care while another task needs full attention | Offer a safe handoff or interruptible step |
| Later scenario has a health warning | Present a reviewed help-seeking decision immediately. Offer a debrief after it |

## 8. Older children

Age changes the communication. Young toddlers may cry, cling or resist a transition. Older children can describe a need or start a conversation. Examples include a tantrum on leaving the park, a preschool drop-off and a school-age nightmare. Choose a response appropriate to the need, such as helping with a transition, acknowledging a feeling, offering a choice, keeping a boundary or listening. Debrief how the response affected the scene.
