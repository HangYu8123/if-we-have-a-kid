# 00 — Vision & Goals

> Status: Draft v0.2 · 2026-11-17

## 1. The experience

Children Simulator is a real-time Android caregiving game for people who are considering becoming parents or want to practice care. The child communicates through behavior, responds to the quality of care, and gradually grows. A player feels what an ordinary day asks of them, learns what to look for, and reflects on the kind of support they would need.

## 2. Audience

| Audience | Use |
|---|---|
| Adults considering children | Experience repeated demands, interruptions and how much time care takes |
| Expecting parents and other caregivers | Practice reading cues and carrying out everyday care |
| Partners and households | Try shifts and talk through expectations, handoffs and workloads |
| Adults exploring care at different ages | Play a later-stage chapter once it is available |

The initial audience is adults. Classroom use and teen players require separate design work.

## 3. Design principles

- **Care takes time.** A feed, change or settling session has steps and elapsed time. Two ordinary tasks can overlap when the child wakes.
- **Observe before acting.** Cues can have several explanations. Recent care and a quick check help a player choose what to try. Safe actions can help even when the child continues crying.
- **Learn while playing.** A short explanation follows an unfamiliar event. Practice and a knowledge check reinforce the same skill. Health and safety teaching uses direct sources and review.
- **Grow through changing responsibilities.** A newborn needs hands-on care day and night. Older children bring supervision, routines, logistics and conversation.
- **Match the player's circumstances.** Realism controls workload and night alerts. Pausing and accepting help are visible choices. A safe handoff can teach how much another caregiver took on.
- **Build one child first.** Validate the everyday care loop before adding siblings, complex health episodes or connected households.

## 4. Scope

The **48-hour Newborn Weekend Trial** starts with a single three-week-old and practices feeding, diapers, sleep and soothing. A helper handles designated shifts. The **Practice Room** revisits the core tasks. Later, **Life Journey** follows monthly then yearly chapters, with scenarios and support for multiple children. See [12](12-roadmap-and-open-questions.md).

## 5. What success looks like

| Area | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Experience | Players can describe recurring responsibilities and a realistic plan for help after the trial |
| Learning | A short quiz and replay capture changes in cue-reading and in care decisions |
| Cadence | Seeded game days hit meaningful feed, diaper and sleep balance bands for each released age |
| Device behavior | Physical-device tests record alert timing and fallbacks under common system settings |
| Accessibility | Essential actions can be completed with touch, larger type, TalkBack and silent cues |
| Reflection | Reports describe care, interruptions and help handled during the simulation. Parenting readiness remains a personal decision |

## 6. Tone and safety

Show the child as a person with a range of temperaments and needs. Respect family structures and feeding choices. Give mistakes clear coaching and the option to try again. Use safe intervention and handoff for virtual distress, with no graphic injury or child death. Require an opt-in per window for audible overnight interruptions and make pause and handoff easy to find. See [05](05-realism-levels-and-notifications.md) and [09](09-education-curriculum.md).
