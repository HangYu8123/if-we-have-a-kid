## 1. How needs work

A need has a stable ID, a driver, age and capability gates, observable cues, actions, and a teaching link where relevant. The first trial builds hunger (N01), wet or dirty diapers (N05/N06), sleep (N08), and comfort (including N20/N22/N24). Gas (N04) can change how a feed finishes. The other rows describe later care, health and household scenarios.

A recurring routine progresses from **latent → emerging → active → urgent**. An active need becomes eligible for an alert when the player is on duty. Correct care or a helper can resolve it. A temporary comfort action can lower distress while the underlying need remains open. Overlapping needs share care groups when appropriate and keep separate need IDs. See [04](04-crying-and-soothing.md) and [05](05-realism-levels-and-notifications.md).

A safety concern takes an authored prevention or help-seeking path. Health scenarios and later teaching details require source and review records. Reference durations in the action tables are game estimates. Care sessions use real elapsed time, scaled by the selected realism preset.

## 3. Cue vocabulary: what the child "says"

Many cues have **more than one meaning on purpose**. The player has to combine cues with **context** (time since the last feed, nap or diaper; recent events) and **checks** (diaper check, touching the chest for temperature, offering a finger to test sucking). This is the core "reading your child" skill ([04](04-crying-and-soothing.md)). In the app, checks are touches: the face (rooting toward a finger), a hand (grasp, fists), the tummy (gas), the diaper and the feet. Cue text appears only after a check ([10](10-game-design-and-scoring.md) §5.1).

| Cue | Ages | Possible meanings |
|---|---|---|
| Rooting (turning head with open mouth when the cheek is touched) | 0–4 mo | Hunger (strong) |
| Sucking on hands or fists | 0–6 mo | Hunger **or** self-soothing |
| Lip smacking, tongue out | 0–6 mo | Hunger |
| Clenched fists | 0–6 mo | Hunger, stress |
| Relaxed, open hands; slower sucking | 0–12 mo | Full / content |
| Turning head away | 0–12 mo | Full (during a feed) **or** overstimulated (during play) |
| Yawning, staring off, quieter | 0–3 y | Tired (early) |
| Eye rubbing | 0–3 y | Tired |
| Ear pulling | 4 mo–3 y | Tired, teething, **or** ear infection (look for fever) |
| Red eyebrows or eyelids | 0–12 mo | Tired |
| Arching back | 0–12 mo | Gas, reflux, overstimulation, frustration, overtired |
| Legs pulled up to the belly, grimace | 0–6 mo | Gas, needs a burp, constipation |
| Jerky movements, hiccups, looking away | 0–6 mo | Overstimulation (hiccups are also simply normal) |
| Squirming and grunting in sleep | 0–3 mo | **Normal active sleep.** Pause before picking up |
| Grunting and straining while awake | 0–3 mo | Pooping (normal "learning to poop") |
| Sneezing, hiccups, noisy breathing | 0–3 mo | Usually normal; **red flags:** fast breathing with chest pulling in, grunting with every breath, blue lips |
| Drooling, chewing everything | 3–30 mo | Normal development or teething |
| Sudden high-pitched cry | All | Pain: check for a hair wrapped around a toe, a clothing tag, a pinch, illness |
| Weak, moaning cry; floppy; very sleepy | All | **Illness — red flag** |
| Clinging, crying when the parent leaves | 6–24 mo | Separation anxiety (normal) |
| Whining | 1–5 y | Hungry, tired, bored, wants connection |
| Pointing | ~12 mo+ | Wants something, or wants you to look (joint attention) |
| Hiding in a corner, going quiet | 1–3 y | Pooping |
| Potty dance, holding crotch | 2–5 y | Needs the toilet *now* |
| "Watch me!", acting out | 2–8 y | Needs attention or recognition |
| Withdrawn, a change in sleep or appetite | 5 y+ | Worry, bullying, illness: start a conversation |

### Cry "types"

Cry intensity is a cue to observe alongside body language and recent care. Use the descriptions below as overlapping presentation examples. Audio, text and animation communicate behavior with the same information.

| Example behavior | Sound and context | Possible related needs |
|---|---|---|
| Building cry with rooting | May follow earlier feeding cues | N01 |
| Fussing and yawning | May appear after a period awake | N08 |
| Discomfort | Fussy, on and off | N04, N05/N06, N09, N10 |
| Boredom / loneliness | Fussing that stops when engaged | N20, N21, N24 |
| Sudden or unusually intense cry | Check cues and context, including whether the child appears unwell | Illness, teething, a hair tourniquet, injury |
| Colic / PURPLE | Intense, long, hard to soothe, in the evening | Colic (no single cause) |
| Unusual cry with other symptoms | Recognize the change from usual behavior | Illness: check for red flags |

## 6. Common mis-steps: built-in teachable moments

Examples below become coaching prompts or blocked choices. The game shows a safe next action and a short explanation. See the [curriculum review rules](09-education-curriculum.md#5-content-standards--review-workflow).

| Mis-step | Simulated consequence | Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Propping the bottle | Block and practice holding the baby and pacing the feed | L-FEED-04 |
| Adding cereal to the bottle "to help sleep" | Not recommended unless medically directed: choking/overfeeding risk; no real sleep gain | L-PAR-09 |
| Microwaving a bottle | Hot-spot burn risk event | L-FEED-03 |
| Watering down formula to stretch it | Dangerous: poor growth, electrolyte imbalance; mandatory lesson | L-FEED-03 |
| Honey before 12 months | Infant botulism risk; the choice is blocked with an explanation | L-FEED-08 |
| Cow's milk as the main drink before 12 months | Iron-deficiency risk | L-FEED-11 |
| Water or juice for a young infant | Water before 6 mo: hyponatremia risk; juice before 12 mo: not recommended | L-FEED-11 |
| Blanket, pillow, bumper or stuffed toy in the crib | Clear the hazards before completing the sleep setup | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Stomach or side sleeping | SIDS-risk lesson | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Scenario about an exhausted caregiver | Discuss handing off or using a safe sleep space | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Letting the baby sleep in a car seat or swing at home | Positional-asphyxia lesson; move to a flat surface | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Swaddling after rolling begins | Safety lesson | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Leaving the baby on a changing table or bed "for a second" | Fall event (from 3–4 months the baby can roll) | L-SAFE-05 |
| Leaving a child in the bath "for a second" | Drowning-risk interrupt; the game will not allow it to complete | L-SAFE-03 |
| Baby walker | Injury-risk lesson (the AAP calls for a ban) | L-SAFE-05 |
| Puffy coat in the car seat | Loose-harness lesson | L-SAFE-01 |
| Turning the car seat forward too early | Lesson | L-SAFE-01 |
| Caregiver overwhelmed by persistent crying | Offer the coping plan and the safe break prompt | L-CRY-04 |
| Benzocaine gel / amber necklace for teething | Blocked; lesson | L-HEALTH-07 |
| Aspirin for fever | Reye's syndrome lesson | L-HEALTH-06 |
| Kitchen spoon for medicine | Dosing-error event | L-HEALTH-06 |
| Screens to soothe an infant | Short-term calm, no long-term benefit; lesson | L-DEV-06 |
| Giving the demanded candy to end a tantrum | Ends this tantrum faster, **raises future tantrum likelihood** | L-BEH-01 |
| Over-bundling | Overheating cues; SIDS-risk factor lesson | L-SLEEP-01 |
| Waiting until the infant is very upset | Show how earlier cues and brief comfort can help the next attempt | L-FEED-01 |
| Feeding at every cry | Spit-up and discomfort when the cause was tiredness or gas | L-CRY-01 |
