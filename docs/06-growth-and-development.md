# 06 — Growth & Development: the child grows up gradually

> Status: Draft v0.2 · 2026-11-17
> Covers request item 5: *"the baby can grow up gradually"*.
> Related: [01 Age & time model](01-age-and-time-model.md) (how fast age moves) · [03 Frequency tables](03-realistic-frequency-tables.md) (how needs change) · [07 Scenarios](07-interactions-and-scenarios.md) (what happens at each stage)

As the development clock moves ([01](01-age-and-time-model.md)), **six things grow together**:

| What grows | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| 1. **Needs profile** | Smoothly interpolated daily rhythm: fewer feeds, consolidated sleep, meals, school ([03](03-realistic-frequency-tables.md)) |
| 2. **Body** | Weight, length/height, head size, teeth; clothes and diapers outgrown |
| 3. **Abilities** | Milestones: smiling, rolling, crawling, walking, talking. Each ability unlocks **new needs and new risks** |
| 4. **Personality** | Temperament from birth, plus traits shaped (gently) by experience |
| 5. **Relationship** | The bond with each caregiver |
| 6. **Look & voice** | Art and audio change by age band; the Memory Book fills up |

---

## 1. Physical growth

Move artwork and sizing along a seeded growth path. Later interactions can include outgrowing clothes or diapers, a change to a new sleep space, or reviewing the limits for a car seat. A checkup can explain what a growth chart is using a fictional example. Use reviewed region-specific examples and avoid calculating medical risk from player actions.

## 2. Developmental milestones

A capability can emerge within a stage and trigger a cue, a new interaction or a safety change. Examples include a first social smile and turn-taking, attempts to roll, reaching for toys, using a cup with help, first words and longer conversations. Later stages add skills such as getting dressed, joining a conversation or taking on a small responsibility.

Use the CDC age checkpoints through five years as a source for teaching. The checklists describe what most children can do by a given age and support developmental monitoring. Concerns about a real child's development belong in a conversation with a qualified professional. [CDC milestone checklists](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/resources/milestones-checklist-by-age.html), [CDC developmental monitoring and screening](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/about/developmental-monitoring-and-screening.html)

The age bands in the game give each capability variation. A child can use different ways to explore or communicate. Some capabilities, such as crawling, can be skipped. Ability to access food or a sleep setting depends on its specific eligibility conditions.

## 3. Abilities unlock new needs and new risks

This is the core "it gets easier, then different" loop.

| Ability | Illustrative game window | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| Social smile | 6–8 weeks | Serve-and-return play; emotional reward moments |
| Reaching, grasping, mouthing | 3–6 mo | Choking hazards (small objects); hair-grabbing; toy-safety checks |
| **Rolling** | 4–6 mo (first *attempts* often 2–4 mo) | **Stop swaddling at the first signs**; falls from beds and changing tables; diaper-change wrestling |
| Sitting | 6–9 mo | High chair (with straps); solids |
| **Crawling** | 6–10 mo (some skip it) | **Childproofing hunt**; gates; floor hazards (cords, outlets, small objects, pet food, plants) |
| Pincer grasp | 9–12 mo | Finger foods; small-object choking risk rises |
| Pulling to stand / cruising | 9–12 mo | Lower the crib mattress; furniture tip-over risk; table-edge bumps |
| **Walking** | 9–15 mo (average ~12) | **Toddler supervision model**; stairs; outdoor and parking-lot safety; drowning risk (pools, buckets) |
| Climbing | 15–24 mo | Window falls (window stops); anchor furniture; climbing out of the crib → toddler bed |
| Opening doors and containers | 18–30 mo | Locks; poisoning risk rises (medicines, detergent pods, button batteries) |
| Language: words → sentences | 12–36 mo | Dialogue choices; "no!"; tantrums **decrease** as language grows |
| Toilet awareness | 18–36 mo | Potty system |
| Pretend play | 18–36 mo | New play options; imaginary friends |
| Tricycle / bike | 3–6 y | Helmet; road safety |
| Reading | 5–7 y | Reading practice; homework |
| Independence | 8–12 y | Phone, online safety, staying home alone, walking to school |
| Puberty | ~8–14 y | Body talks, hygiene, privacy, mood |

## 4. Temperament

Seed variation in regularity, intensity, response to gentle soothing, adaptation to a change and sensitivity to noise or stimulation. Those settings modify when a cue appears or how the virtual child responds. Explain the behavior in context, for example “Loud rooms can be tiring for this child.” Practice scenarios can choose a behavior to demonstrate. Avoid tagging a child as easy or difficult.

## 5. Familiarity and connection

A familiar caregiver may elicit more relaxed reactions or a different greeting. Build that familiarity with shared play and care. Track it per caregiver. Help-seeking, scheduled coverage and missed Android alerts leave it intact. Give reunions and repair after a difficult moment room to happen.

## 6. How the player supports development

Offer play and care activities that make space for practice. Awake, supervised floor play, responsive talking, shared reading and giving an older child time to try are examples. Record these opportunities and show responses within a scene. The seed and capability schedule determine when milestones appear. A checkup scene can teach how to raise a concern with a clinician.

## 7. Transitions: decisions the player makes as the child grows

| Transition | Typical timing | In-game trigger | Player's decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop swaddling | First signs of trying to roll (often 2–4 mo) | Rolling-attempt cue | Must stop (enforced, with a lesson) |
| Move to own room | Room-sharing recommended for at least the first 6 months | Option unlocks at 6 mo | Yes |
| Start solids | Around 6 months, with readiness signs | Reviewed readiness cues and age gate | Start practice when the signs appear |
| Drop night feeds | Varies by child | Longer stretches in the age-based routine | Discuss feeding changes with a clinician in a later lesson |
| 3 → 2 naps | 6–9 mo | Nap-resistance cues | Adapt the schedule |
| Bottle → cup; formula or breast milk → whole milk | Family meal routine from around 12 months | Cup practice and meal slots. Continued breast/chestfeeding can remain available | Choose the feeding route and discussed transitions |
| 2 → 1 nap | 12–18 mo | Nap refusal | Adapt the schedule |
| Pacifier weaning | Varies; dentists advise stopping by ~3 years | Option from ~12 mo | Yes |
| Crib → bed | When the child can climb out or reaches the product limit | Climbing cue or equipment check | Select a suitable sleep space |
| Potty training | Readiness between ~18 months and 3 years | Readiness signs | Method and timing |
| Drop the nap | 3–5 y | Bedtime resistance | Adapt |
| Daycare / preschool | Family-dependent; preschool ~3–4 y | Player's schedule | Yes |
| Kindergarten | 5–6 y | Regional school calendar | Automatic |
| Forward-facing car seat | Only after outgrowing the seat's rear-facing limits | Seat limit reached | Correct timing |
| Booster seat | After outgrowing the harness; until the seat belt fits (~145 cm / 4'9", often 8–12 y) | Seat limit reached | Correct timing |
| Front seat | 13 y | Age | Yes |
| First phone / staying home alone | Family decision and local law | Requests start ~8–10 y | Agreement and rules |

## 8. Look & voice

- **Art bands** (character models with age-appropriate animation sets): newborn (curled, sleepy) → young infant (head control, smiles) → sitter → crawler → early walker → toddler → preschooler → school-age → tween → teen.
- **Every band has:** idle, sleeping (active vs. quiet sleep), each cue animation (rooting, yawning, eye rubbing, arching, legs pulled up), crying levels, feeding (latched / bottle / spoon), burping, bath, play. Plus band-specific ones: rolling, crawling, cruising, walking, tantrum, potty, school bag.
- **Customization:** name; sex (or "surprise me"); skin tone; hair color and texture; eye color; optional resemblance to the parent avatars.
- **Voice progression** (recorded audio sets): newborn cries → coos (~6–8 weeks) → laughs (~3–4 months) → babbling (~6 months) → "mama/dada"-type syllables (8–12 months) → first words (~12 months) → two-word phrases (~2 years) → sentences (~3 years). The child calls the player by the label the player chooses (Mama, Papa, Mommy, Daddy, Baba, Mum, or others from a list).

## 9. Memory Book (the emotional reward)

Captured automatically, and exportable as images:
- **Firsts:** smile, laugh, roll, tooth, food, crawl, step, word, birthday, day at daycare or school, lost tooth.
- **Monthly illustrated portrait** (then yearly), growth chart, handprint/footprint art.
- **Care and connection:** an evening when a helper made a difference, a favorite song or a first shared book.

## 10. Later phase: Preemie mode & special circumstances

- **Preemie mode:** born at 32–36 weeks, NICU discharge, **corrected age** for milestones, more frequent feeds, stricter infection precautions, temperature regulation. Needs neonatal-expert review.
- **Twins** can start with two modeled full-term infants. Earlier birth and NICU follow-up can be authored with neonatal expertise in a later phase. See [08](08-multiple-children.md).
- **Medical conditions** (reflux/GERD, cow's-milk protein allergy, eczema; later developmental differences) can be added only with expert input and careful, respectful design.
