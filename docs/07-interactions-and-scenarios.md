# Part A — Core interaction mechanics

The first trial uses M01–M06, M08–M09 and M12/M12b, with brief awake play and the break controls described in [04](04-crying-and-soothing.md). The remaining rows are design candidates. The trial uses touch and accessible controls. Optional sensors can map to these actions later.

Work through care steps and respond to cues over elapsed time. Show what the player did, what changed and where to find a safe next step. Use reviewed lessons whenever a mechanic teaches health or safety.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

# Part B — Situations by life stage

Each table: **ID · Situation · Trigger / frequency · Good handling (→ lesson) · Common mistakes → result**. [03](03-realistic-frequency-tables.md) informs the cadence. Realism changes exposure and alerts. The rows below are candidates for phased delivery.

| ID | Situation | Trigger / frequency | Good handling | Common mistakes → result |

# Part C — Cross-age systems

## C1. Illness scenarios

Model the routines illness disrupts and who can help. Each scenario has a virtual symptom timeline, support options and a reviewed help-seeking choice. Medical and emergency cases are opt-in practice. The regular first trial focuses on routine care.

| Group | Candidate scenarios |
|---|---|
| Newborn health | Fever, jaundice, poor feeding or eye symptoms |
| Respiratory | A cold, RSV, flu or croup disrupts a feed and sleep |
| Digestion | Vomiting, diarrhea, constipation or hydration concerns |
| Skin, mouth and teeth | Diaper rash, cradle cap, eczema, thrush, lice and teething |
| Other later topics | Ear infection, sore throat, hand-foot-and-mouth, fever with rash or suspected allergy |

Use approved content for symptom detail and urgency. In a newborn scenario, a rectal temperature of 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher calls for immediate medical contact. [AAP guidance on fever in young infants](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/health-issues/conditions/fever/pages/fever-and-your-baby.aspx)

## C2. First-aid awareness (M40)

Author separate, untimed practice for choking and breathing emergencies, head injuries, burns, suspected poisoning, swallowed batteries or magnets, water emergencies, allergic reactions, seizures, and a missing child. Use age-specific, reviewed regional instructions. Practice recognizing trouble, getting help and finding qualified training. [AHA pediatric basic life support](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support)

## C3. Supervision and injury scenarios

Introduce household hazards when capabilities emerge. A child who pulls up can reach a previously inaccessible object. Give the player a chance to intervene, move to a safe space or hand off. Keep supervision responsibility in the virtual state when switching screens or addressing another child.

## C7. Seasons, holidays & travel

- **Summer:** heat and hydration; sunscreen (≥ 6 mo) and shade; **hot cars**; pool season (L-SAFE-03, L-SAFE-09).
- **Winter:** layers; **no puffy coats in car seats**; RSV and flu season; dry skin (L-SAFE-01, L-HEALTH-04).
- **Holidays:** overstimulation, late nights, gift overload, hazards (ornaments, candles, button batteries, small toy parts, alcohol left out).
- **Daylight saving time:** keep elapsed intervals and re-evaluate local appointments ([01](01-age-and-time-model.md) §6).
- **Travel:** packing, car seats on planes, feeding during takeoff and landing, jet lag, unchildproofed hotels and relatives' homes.
- **Celebrations:** birthdays, first holidays, a family wedding with a toddler.

# Part D — Scenario authoring format

## First-trial example

```yaml
id: SC-S0-41
title: One wake, two needs
stage: S0
mode: trial_fixture
ageDays: 21
trigger:
  type: wake_opened
  fixtureLocalTime: "03:12"
  needs: [N01, N06]
  sameWakeGroup: true
cues: [rooting, squirming, crying]
choices:
  - action: M01
    outcome: comfort_with_needs_still_open
  - action: M06
    outcome: diaper_changed
  - action: M03
    requires: bottle_route
    outcome: milk_need_resolved_at_fullness
  - action: M04
    requires: breast_or_chest_route
    outcome: milk_need_resolved_at_fullness
  - action: M12
    requires: safe_sleep_setup
    outcome: settled_if_ready
  - action: handoff
    outcome: helper_care_logged
lessons: [L-FEED-01, L-HYG-01, L-SLEEP-01]
reviewStatus: draft
```

- **Player on duty.** Open two needs and one waking. Attempt at most one alert for the wake. Picking up can ease crying. Changing resolves the diaper need. Feeding ends at fullness. A safe transfer finishes the settling sequence.
- **Helper on duty.** Resolve both needs in the helper's log and suppress the player's alert.
- **Delayed delivery.** Skip expired or resolved groups. Tapping an old notice opens the timeline or current care.
- **Interruption.** Pause during the feed, reopen and resume at the saved step. A handoff records which caregiver did each step.

For an illustrative Realistic run, use reference durations of 3 minutes for the diaper, 4 minutes for bottle prep, 15 minutes for the feed and 5 minutes for settling. At the preset's 60% effort scale, the four actions take about 16 hands-on minutes. An early fullness cue can finish the feed sooner. The same wake can take longer when the player tries to soothe first or needs a handoff.

## Other authoring requirements

Each scenario has a unique ID, age and capability requirements, a trigger, cooldown or repeat rules, care or help outcomes, and a debrief. Use fixtures for timing and state transitions. Health and safety teaching needs direct sources, region, content version and review. See [09](09-education-curriculum.md) §5.

For each new age, exercise the ordinary routine, evening or nighttime care, play or connection, a handoff, an interruption and an age-related task. For a second child, exercise simultaneous routine needs, an urgent concern while hands are occupied, and one grouped wake across both children. See [08](08-multiple-children.md).
